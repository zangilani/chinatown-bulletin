Today's Briefing今日焦點

Excavation deepens at the jail site as the neighborhood pushes its housing alternative

Crews have moved into below-grade excavation and pilings work for the $3.9 billion, 16-story detention tower at 125 White Street — what would be the tallest jail in the world — with foundation work expected to continue through the fall toward a projected 2032 completion. Neighbors United Below Canal and Welcome to Chinatown are still pressing their alternative: roughly 1,040 units of affordable housing, a public plaza, and community retail on the same site, a plan now backed by Borough President Mark Levine, Assemblymember Grace Lee, and Council Members Marte, Powers, Won, Krishnan, and Lee. Separately, Chinatown Partnership is opening applications for a new Building Upgrade Fund offering $10,000–$100,000 grants toward storefront and accessibility improvements, with a launch event at the Italian American Museum this Friday.

On the culture calendar, Think!Chinatown turns ten tonight with an archival screening and animated-short premiere at DCTV, ahead of its Mosco Street Block Party on August 30 with hands-on linocut printmaking. Yu & Me Books rounds out the month with an August 20 author talk and an August 21 open mic hosted by Ed Lin. On the food side, keep an eye out for Cavalier, a new Mediterranean spot from the Diner/Monkey Bar team landing at Kimlau Square in late August, and mark your calendar for Send Chinatown Love's month-long "Passport to Chinatown" food crawl kicking off in September across 13 local restaurants and bakeries.
